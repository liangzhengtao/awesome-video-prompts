## Forests & Woodlands

### Ancient Redwood Grove
**Prompt:** Camera tilts up from the forest floor — ferns, moss-covered roots, a fallen log hosting a colony of mushrooms. The tilt continues: past the mid-canopy where filtered light creates a green cathedral. Woodpecker holes dot the bark. A Douglas squirrel clings to the trunk, watching. The tilt reaches the crown — 300 feet up — where the redwood's top catches full sunlight. Fog drifts through the canopy. A red-tailed hawk circles in the gap between the trees and the sky. The camera completes the tilt and is looking straight up at the crown against a pale blue sky. The scale is impossible — this tree was here before Rome fell.

**Settings:** 9:16 (vertical), 5 seconds

**Expected result:** The vertical journey from forest floor to canopy crown reveals the full scale of ancient redwoods. The fog, wildlife, and filtered light create a sacred space. The hawk at the top completes the journey.

**Variations:**
- Bamboo forest: rapid upward tilt through bamboo stalks, green light
- Boreal forest: snow, evergreens, a moose in the mist
- Tropical rainforest: dense canopy, vines, parrots, humidity visible

### Morning Mist in Birch Forest
**Prompt:** A white birch forest at dawn. Mist hangs at waist height — the white birch trunks emerge from the mist like columns in a Greek temple. Camera moves forward through the forest at a slow walking pace. Each trunk passes on either side — the repetition creates a hypnotic rhythm. Sunlight breaks through the canopy in isolated shafts, illuminating patches of mist. A deer stands in one shaft of light — motionless, watching the camera. Its breath is visible. It flicks an ear, then walks away, disappearing into the mist. The camera continues forward. The mist thickens. The forest becomes less visible. We are inside a cloud.

**Settings:** 16:9, 5 seconds

**Expected result:** Ethereal birch forest experience. The mist creates layers — visible, half-visible, invisible. The deer encounter is a brief wildlife moment that doesn't overstay. The final thickening of mist is the surrender to nature.

**Variations:**
- Autumn birch: golden leaves, fallen leaf carpet, different warmth
- Snow-covered: white on white, minimal, stark beauty
- Night: moonlight through birch trunks, owls, silver and shadow

### Forest Floor Ecosystem
**Prompt:** Macro lens, ground level, in a Pacific Northwest old-growth forest. A world in miniature: moss covers everything like green velvet. Tiny mushrooms (fly agaric — red with white dots) push through leaf litter. A banana slug (5 inches long, bright yellow) slides across a mossy log, leaving a slime trail. Springtails (tiny arthropods) hop between moss fronds. A centipede navigates the terrain. Under the log: a salamander (Pacific giant) waits, motionless, its red belly visible. Water droplets on moss fronds are perfect spheres. The camera drifts forward slowly. The sound design: dripping water, the faint buzz of insects, the hum of decomposition.

**Settings:** 16:9, 5 seconds

**Expected result:** A complete ecosystem at macro scale. Each organism has its role. The moss creates a uniform green backdrop that makes each creature pop. David Attenborough narration optional but expected.

**Variations:**
- Tropical floor: leaf-cutter ants, poison dart frogs, decaying fruit
- Desert floor: scorpion under a rock, lizard, tumbleweed
- Cave floor: blind cave fish, stalactite drips, bioluminescent fungi

## Oceans & Water

### Coral Spawning Night
**Prompt:** Underwater time-lapse on the Great Barrier Reef during coral spawning. Millions of pink and white egg-sperm bundles rise from coral colonies like underwater snow. The reef — normally static — is actively releasing its reproductive material. The bundles are the size of rice grains but collectively create a blizzard. Moonlight filters from the surface, creating silver-blue light shafts. Plankton-feeding fish dart through the clouds of spawn, feasting. A manta ray glides through the background, enormous and serene. The camera: wide angle, steady, capturing the scale of this annual event. The spawn rises, rises, rises toward the moon.

**Settings:** 16:9, 5 seconds

**Expected result:** A rare natural phenomenon captured at scale. The "underwater snowfall" of coral spawning is one of nature's most spectacular events. The manta ray adds scale and grace. The moonlight adds magic.

**Variations:**
- Kelp forest: sun rays through giant kelp, leopard sharks, sea otters
- Deep sea: bioluminescence, anglerfish, complete darkness except life
- Mangrove: above and below waterline, roots as habitat, juvenile fish

### Wave Patterns
**Prompt:** Aerial (drone) shot of ocean waves breaking on a coastline with unusual rock formations. The waves interact with the rocks — wrapping around them, creating complex interference patterns. White foam against deep blue-green water. The patterns are mathematical: each wave follows the same rules, but the rock formations create infinite variation. Camera slowly rotates from straight-down to a 45-degree angle, revealing the 3D nature of the wave-rock interaction. A flock of seabirds (gannets) dive into a bait ball visible in the clear water — white splashes against blue. The coastline stretches to the horizon.

**Settings:** 16:9, 5 seconds

**Expected result:** Aerial wave art — the intersection of physics and geology creates natural generative art. The gannet dive-bombing adds wildlife action to an abstract water pattern. Beautiful from any angle.

**Variations:**
- Tsunami warning: dark, powerful waves, no foam, ominous
- Frozen lake: ice patterns formed by the same wave physics, static
- River rapids: white water, boulders, kayaker for scale

### Mangrove Roots at Low Tide
**Prompt:** The root system of a red mangrove at low tide. The tangled prop roots are exposed — they create a labyrinthine structure above the waterline. Barnacles, oysters, and algae coat every surface. A mudskipper (amphibious fish) climbs a root using its pectoral fins — it's out of the water, breathing air. Fiddler crabs scuttle across the mud between the roots — the males wave their oversized claws. The water between the roots is shallow, warm, and teeming with juvenile fish. The camera: water level, looking through the root network at the setting sun. The roots create a natural frame. Tide is coming in — the water rises slowly, covering the mud, submerging the crab holes.

**Settings:** 16:9, 5 seconds

**Expected result:** The mangrove as a bridge between land and sea. The exposed root system at low tide reveals a hidden world of amphibious creatures. The rising tide creates urgency. Golden hour through the roots is the hero shot.

**Variations:**
- High tide: same mangrove, fully submerged, different inhabitants
- Night: bioluminescent plankton in the mangrove channels
- Aerial: the mangrove canopy from above, green against blue water

## Mountains & Peaks

### Sunrise from Everest Base Camp
**Prompt:** Time-lapse from Everest Base Camp. Pre-dawn: the sky is deep indigo, stars visible. The first light touches the summit of Everest (visible in the distance, 12,000 feet above) — it glows orange while everything below is still in shadow. This is the "golden pyramid" effect. The light descends the mountain as the sun rises: summit → upper slopes → Khumbu Icefall → Base Camp. The ice formations of the Khumbu Icefall catch the light — each serac, each crevasse edge becomes gold. Prayer flags in the foreground flutter in the wind, their colors (blue, white, red, green, yellow) catching the new light. Climbers' tents begin to glow as occupants wake. The camera: fixed, wide angle, capturing the full light progression.

**Settings:** 16:9, 5 seconds

**Expected result:** The most dramatic light show on Earth — sunrise hitting the highest peaks while the valleys remain dark. The golden summit of Everest is the hero frame. The prayer flags add human and spiritual context.

**Variations:**
- Alps: Mont Blanc sunrise, different scale, green valleys below
- Patagonia: Torres del Paine, granite towers catching first light
- Japanese Alps: snow-capped peaks above cloud inversion layer

### Avalanche Dust Cloud
**Prompt:** A cornice breaks on a mountain ridge and releases a slab avalanche. The camera is positioned on a safe opposing slope, telephoto. The initial slab maintains its form for a moment, then disintegrates into powder. The powder cloud billows — it's denser than the snow itself, expanding outward and upward. The cloud overtakes the sliding debris, creating a mushroom-shaped explosion of snow. The sound arrives (delayed): a deep roar, then silence. The cloud settles, revealing the debris field — the slope is transformed. The slide path is carved through previously untouched snow. A lone pine tree stands at the margin, half-buried. The mountain is quiet again.

**Settings:** 16:9, 5 seconds

**Expected result:** Nature's raw destructive power captured. The powder cloud's expansion is the visual spectacle. The silence after the roar is the emotional weight. The lone pine is the survivor.

**Variations:**
- Ice calving: glacier face collapsing into ocean, waves, spray
- Rockfall: boulders tumbling down a cliff face, dust, echoes
- Lahar: volcanic mudflow, unstoppable, consuming everything

### Cloud Inversion
**Prompt:** View from a mountain summit above a cloud inversion layer. The clouds fill the valleys below like a white ocean — perfectly flat surface, undulating slightly. Mountain peaks poke through the clouds like islands. The camera is at the summit — a small rocky outcrop — looking down at the cloud sea. The sun is above the cloud layer, casting shadows of the island-peaks onto the white surface. The clouds move slowly — waves of cloud, like a slow-motion sea. An airplane flies below the camera, above the clouds — its shadow races across the white surface. The camera: wide angle, slight downward tilt, capturing the surreal landscape of being above the weather.

**Settings:** 16:9, 5 seconds

**Expected result:** The most surreal mountain phenomenon — standing above the clouds. The cloud sea with mountain islands creates an alien landscape. The airplane shadow is the scale reference. Otherworldly.

**Variations:**
- Fog bow: rainbow in the cloud layer, rare and beautiful
- Brocken spectre: climber's shadow cast on the cloud, surrounded by a glory ring
- Sunset: golden light on the cloud sea, mountains as dark silhouettes

## Wildlife

### Cheetah Hunt
**Prompt:** A cheetah explodes from a standstill into full sprint — 0 to 70 mph in 3 seconds. The camera tracks alongside at full speed (vehicle-mounted, stabilized). Every muscle is visible: the spine flexing like a spring, the legs extending fully, the claws gripping the ground (no retracting at this speed). Dust kicks up behind. The prey (a Thomson's gazelle) makes a sharp turn — the cheetah matches, its entire body tilting at 45 degrees, tail acting as a counterbalance. The chase is over in seconds. The camera: telephoto compressed perspective, making the background a blur of golden grass. The savanna is the arena.

**Settings:** 16:9, 5 seconds

**Expected result:** The fastest animal on Earth at full speed. The camera matching the sprint creates visceral immersion. The body mechanics — spine flex, claw grip, tail counterbalance — are the detail. "Planet Earth" quality.

**Variations:**
- Peregrine falcon: diving at 240 mph, prey in frame, compressed perspective
- Sailfish: underwater chase, bill slashing through baitfish
- Wolf pack: coordinated hunting, different members flanking

### Octopus Camouflage
**Prompt:** Close-up of an octopus resting on a coral reef. Its skin is textured and colored to match the coral — brown, bumpy, invisible. A predator (a moray eel) approaches from the right. The octopus's skin ripples — chromatophores fire in sequence. In 0.5 seconds, the octopus changes: bright red with white rings (warning pattern), then shoots a cloud of black ink. The ink cloud expands — the octopus is gone. The camera follows the ink cloud dissipating. Behind it: the octopus has reappeared 10 feet away, now camouflaged against a different surface — sandy, textured, completely different from the first camouflage. The eel searches. The octopus is invisible again.

**Settings:** 16:9, 5 seconds

**Expected result:** The octopus's superpower demonstrated in sequence: camouflage → warning display → ink escape → re-camouflage. Each step is a different survival strategy. The chromatophore detail is mesmerizing.

**Variations:**
- Cuttlefish: more dramatic color changes, hypnotic patterns, mating display
- Mimic octopus: imitating a lionfish, flatfish, and sea snake in sequence
- Squid: schooling, chromatophores pulsing in synchronization

### Hummingbird Feeding
**Prompt:** Macro slow-motion of a hummingbird (rufous or Anna's) hovering at a trumpet flower. Wings beating at 50 beats per second — visible as a blur, then slowing to show individual strokes. The bird's gorget (throat patch) catches light: green to red to copper depending on angle. Its long beak enters the flower — the tongue (forked) extends into the nectar. The bird drinks. Its body is perfectly still in the air — the only movement is the wings. Pollen dusts its forehead. The bird withdraws and zips away — the camera captures the departure as a blur of motion. A single feather drifts in the wind. The flower bounces back from the bird's weight.

**Settings:** 16:9, 5 seconds

**Expected result:** The impossible physics of hummingbird flight made visible. The wing blur, the hovering stillness, the tongue extension — each detail is a marvel. The single feather at the end is a poetic beat.

**Variations:**
- Bee close-up: pollen baskets loaded, wings visible, landing on a flower
- Butterfly emerging: chrysalis splitting, wings unfolding, first wingbeat
- Dragonfly: iridescent wings, compound eyes visible, hunting in flight

## Insects & Micro Worlds

### Ant Colony Cross-Section
**Prompt:** A cross-section view of an ant colony underground (like a nature documentary illustration). The colony is revealed: chambers connected by tunnels, each with a purpose. The queen chamber: large, central, the queen surrounded by attendants. Nursery chambers: larvae being tended by worker ants. Food storage: seeds, fungus gardens (leafcutter ants). The colony buzzes with activity — ants moving through tunnels, carrying loads, communicating with antennae. The camera: slow zoom through the colony, as if slicing through the earth. Soil roots visible at the edges. The colony extends deeper than expected — dozens of chambers.

**Settings:** 16:9, 5 seconds

**Expected result:** A hidden civilization revealed. The cross-section view makes the invisible visible. Each chamber's purpose is clear from the activity within. The scale — thousands of ants in coordinated activity — is the spectacle.

**Variations:**
- Beehive: hexagonal cells, honey, larvae, waggle dance
- Termite mound: cathedral-like internal structure, ventilation shafts
- Wasp nest: paper construction, larvae in cells, guard wasps at entrance

### Dew Drop World
**Prompt:** Macro shot of a single dew drop on a spider web strand at dawn. The dew drop acts as a lens — inside the drop, an inverted world is visible: the surrounding garden is flipped and compressed into the tiny sphere. The camera pushes in to macro extreme: individual cells of the leaf behind the drop are visible. A tiny insect (a springtail) walks along the web strand toward the drop. It reaches the drop and drinks — its reflection visible in the drop's surface. The drop wobbles from the insect's weight but holds. Morning light catches the drop — it becomes a prism, splitting light into a micro-rainbow on the web strand.

**Settings:** 16:9, 5 seconds

**Expected result:** The universe in a dew drop. Macro photography at its most magical. The inverted world inside the drop, the tiny insect visitor, and the micro-rainbow are three layers of wonder in one frame.

**Variations:**
- Frozen dew: ice crystals forming on the web, geometric patterns
- Raindrop: larger, multiple drops, each containing a different reflection
- Pollen grain: even more macro, individual grain structure visible

## Tips: Describing Natural Phenomena and Animal Behavior

### Describing Natural Phenomena
- **Weather**: "Cumulus clouds building into cumulonimbus — the tower grows visibly"
- **Light**: "Alpenglow — the mountain turns pink as the sun sets behind it"
- **Water**: "The river braids into multiple channels across the gravel delta"
- **Geology**: "Basalt columns, hexagonal, like the Giant's Causeway"
- **Seasons**: "The first frost — crystals forming on grass blades, each one unique"

### Describing Animal Behavior
- **Be specific**: "The fox pounces — a vertical leap, forepaws first, into the snow" not "a fox hunting"
- **Include the aftermath**: "The fox emerges with a vole" — the payoff
- **Social dynamics**: "The alpha female eats first; others wait, ears back"
- **Environmental response**: "The flock lifts as one when the shadow passes overhead"
- **Micro-behaviors**: "The bird ruffles its feathers, then preens one wing" — life between the dramatic moments

### Nature Filming Tips for AI
- **Name the season**: "Early autumn, first color change" — grounds the time
- **Specify weather**: "Overcast with intermittent sun breaks" — sets the light
- **Include scale references**: "A hawk circles overhead — wingspan wider than the river below"
- **Sound design cues**: "Only wind and distant thunder" — affects the visual mood
- **Time of day**: "The blue hour — 20 minutes after sunset" — precise light
