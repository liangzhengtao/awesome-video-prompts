## Elemental Effects

### Fire Morphing
**Prompt:** A human hand made entirely of flame — each finger is a distinct fire tendril, orange at the base, blue at the tips, white-hot at the core. The hand reaches forward and touches a surface of ice. Where flame meets ice: steam erupts. The ice melts in the shape of the handprint. The fire hand withdraws. The handprint glows, then cools. The ice refreezes, but the handprint remains as a permanent scar — melted and refrozen, opaque where the rest is clear. Camera: close-up, macro detail on the flame-ice interaction.

**Settings:** 9:16, 4 seconds

**Expected result:** Element-vs-element drama. Fire and ice meeting creates steam, melt, and transformation. The permanent handprint is the narrative — something was here, something left a mark.

**Variations:**
- Water hand: liquid fingers touching sand, creating glass (desert heat)
- Lightning hand: electric fingers touching a tree, the tree lights up from inside
- Earth hand: stone fingers touching water, creating a small island

### Water Manipulation
**Prompt:** A sphere of water floats in mid-air, perfectly suspended, defying gravity. It's 12 inches in diameter, crystal clear, and light refracts through it creating caustic patterns on the surrounding surfaces. The sphere begins to deform — stretching into a ribbon, then coiling into a helix, then splitting into multiple smaller spheres. Each smaller sphere catches light independently, creating dozens of tiny rainbows. The spheres orbit each other like a solar system. They recombine into the original sphere. The sphere drops — splashing on a surface below. The splash is frozen in time, droplets suspended mid-air, each one a perfect lens.

**Settings:** 9:16, 4 seconds

**Expected result:** Water that behaves like a living thing — forming, deforming, reforming. The caustic light patterns and rainbow refractions are the visual reward. Physics-defying but visually logical.

**Variations:**
- Blood (stylized): dramatic, red tendrils, gothic aesthetic
- Mercury: metallic, heavy, reflective, liquid metal
- Ink: black water, calligraphic shapes, abstract art forming

### Lightning Storm Indoors
**Prompt:** A dark room. A hand raises, palm up. A spark appears at the fingertip — tiny, flickering. It grows: a miniature lightning bolt arcs from fingertip to ceiling. Then another from the other hand. The room fills with branching electricity — bolts seeking ground, hitting metal objects, illuminating everything in stroboscopic flashes. The person's face is lit intermittently: calm, focused, in control. The lightning coalesces into a single ball of electricity floating between their palms. It pulses like a heartbeat. The ball explodes outward — all the bolts retract, absorbed back into the fingertips. Darkness returns. Only a faint ozone glow remains.

**Settings:** 9:16, 4 seconds

**Expected result:** Electric superpower visualization. The branching lightning is chaotic; the ball of lightning is controlled. The push-pull between chaos and control is the drama. The stroboscopic lighting creates a rave-meets-thunderstorm aesthetic.

**Variations:**
- Fire: same setup, flames instead of lightning, warm lighting
- Gravity: objects floating, orbiting the person, defying physics
- Ice: frost spreading from fingertips across the room

## Destruction & Chaos

### Glass Shattering Symphony
**Prompt:** A wine glass falls from a table in extreme slow motion (1000fps equivalent). It tumbles once, catching light in a prism. It hits the floor — the impact is silent for 0.5 seconds, then the sound arrives: a crystalline CRACK. The glass doesn't just break — it explodes into a thousand fragments. Each shard is individually tracked: they catch light, spinning and tumbling, creating a constellation of reflections. The wine (red) is still inside the ghost shape of the glass for a moment, then collapses into a splash. The shards scatter across the floor, each one a tiny mirror reflecting the room. Camera: ground level, wide angle, catching the full explosion.

**Settings:** 16:9, 4 seconds

**Expected result:** The most beautiful destruction possible. The glass shattering is choreographed like a ballet. Each shard has its own trajectory. The wine's brief ghost-shape is the "wow" moment.

**Variations:**
- Mirror: self-reflection shattering, each shard showing a different version
- Ice sculpture: a swan or dragon shattering, more dramatic shape
- Ceramic vase: different fracture pattern, more substantial pieces

### Building Collapse (Miniature)
**Prompt:** A detailed miniature city (model train scale) sits on a table. A giant hand enters the frame — a child's hand. The hand pushes over a building. In slow motion, the building falls — but as it falls, it transitions from miniature model to real-scale building. By the time it hits the "ground," it's a full-size concrete structure. Dust clouds billow realistically. The hand is now tiny compared to the destruction. The perspective shift — miniature to real — happens during the fall. The child's hand enters again and sets the building back upright. It shrinks back to miniature scale. The cycle resets.

**Settings:** 16:9, 4 seconds

**Expected result:** Scale-shifting destruction — toy becomes real during the fall. The perspective change is the visual trick. The reset at the end adds playful innocence to destruction.

**Variations:**
- Domino chain: miniature dominoes that become full-size walls
- Sand castle: kicked over, becomes a real castle crumbling
- Card house: blown down, becomes a real house in a storm

### Time Reverse — Coffee Chaos
**Prompt:** A knocked-over coffee mug — dark liquid spreading across white paperwork. In reverse: the coffee retreats, pulling back from the paper, rising up off the table, flowing back INTO the mug in a perfect column. The mug rights itself. The paper dries — stains disappearing. Sugar cubes reassemble from scattered granules. A spoon flies from the table back into the saucer. The coffee is now pristine, steam rising, as if the spill never happened. But one detail remains: a single coffee ring stain on the table that didn't reverse. A permanent reminder of the chaos.

**Settings:** 16:9, 4 seconds

**Expected result:** The satisfaction of time-reversal applied to everyday disaster. Everything goes back to normal — except one tiny detail that proves it happened. The single stain is the poetic anchor.

**Variations:**
- Paint spill: all colors retreating to their cans
- Domino fall: dominoes standing back up in reverse order
- Paper shredder: strips reassembling into a document

## Transformation & Morphing

### Object to Animal Morph
**Prompt:** A pocket watch on a table. The watch's gears become visible — spinning, clicking. The gears start to multiply and rearrange. The watch face stretches — becoming a face. The gears become feathers. The chain becomes a tail. The watch transforms into a mechanical bird — steampunk, golden, intricate. The bird's wings unfold (they're clock hands). It takes flight — each wingbeat releases tiny gear-shaped particles. The bird flies upward, catches a sunbeam, and its golden surface gleams. It circles once and flies toward camera. Close-up of its eye: it's a tiny clock face, ticking.

**Settings:** 9:16, 4 seconds

**Expected result:** Steampunk morph — watch becomes bird. Each transformation step is visible: gears → face → feathers → wings. The clock-eye at the end is the detail that sells the concept. Whimsical, mechanical, alive.

**Variations:**
- Book to butterfly: pages becoming wings, text becoming wing patterns
- Pencil to snake: wood grain becoming scales, eraser becoming the head
- Teapot to elephant: spout becomes trunk, handle becomes tail

### Human to Nature
**Prompt:** A person stands in profile. Starting from their feet, they begin to transform into a tree. Skin becomes bark — the texture spreads upward, crack by crack, ridge by ridge. Arms become branches — fingers elongating into twigs. Hair becomes leaves — green, autumnal, then green again as the transformation completes. The person's silhouette is preserved — you can still see the human shape in the tree's form. The camera circles the now-tree-person. A bird lands on a branch (formerly a hand). Roots spread from where the feet were. The tree sways in a breeze that wasn't there before. The person's expression: peaceful, accepting, eyes closed — now knotholes.

**Settings:** 16:9, 4 seconds

**Expected result:** Body horror meets natural beauty. The transformation is gradual enough to be beautiful, not disturbing. The preserved human silhouette in the tree shape is the key image. Mythological — Daphne and Apollo.

**Variations:**
- Human to ocean: skin becomes water surface, body dissolves into waves
- Human to constellation: body becomes starfield, outline preserved in stars
- Human to city: skin becomes concrete, hair becomes antenna arrays

### Season Cycle
**Prompt:** A single oak tree in a field, camera locked, no movement. The tree transforms through all four seasons in 4 seconds. Spring: buds appear, green emerges, grass grows. Summer: full canopy, lush, a butterfly passes. Autumn: leaves turn orange, red, yellow — they begin to fall. Winter: bare branches, snow accumulates, the field turns white. Back to spring: the cycle restarts seamlessly. Each transition is smooth — colors blending, not cutting. The sky changes with the seasons: bright blue → warm haze → grey overcast → white sky. The field follows: wildflowers → tall grass → fallen leaves → snow.

**Settings:** 16:9, 4 seconds (seamless loop)

**Expected result:** The classic season cycle — nature's loop. The smoothness of each transition is the technical achievement. The seamless loop means it can play forever. Meditative, beautiful, universal.

**Variations:**
- Day-to-night: same scene, sun moving across sky, lights coming on
- Growth cycle: seed → sprout → plant → flower → seed
- Decay to renewal: abandoned building → ruins → nature reclaiming → forest

## Supernatural & Fantasy

### Portal Opening
**Prompt:** A person reaches out and "grabs" the air — their fingers dig into something invisible. They pull — the air tears. Behind the tear: a different world — blue sky, floating islands, light that doesn't match the current room. The tear widens into a portal — edges crackling with energy (white-blue sparks, electric). The portal's edges are unstable — they flicker, distort, warp the view of both worlds. Wind rushes through the portal into the current room. Papers fly. The person steps through. The portal closes — the tear seals itself with a zip-like motion, leaving only a brief afterimage. The room returns to normal. Except: a single alien flower petal is on the floor.

**Settings:** 9:16, 4 seconds

**Expected result:** The portal opening is a physical act — tearing reality like fabric. The wind rush and the unstable edges make it feel real. The alien petal left behind is the proof it happened.

**Variations:**
- Mirror portal: touching a mirror, the glass becomes liquid, stepping through
- Door portal: a normal door opens to somewhere impossible
- Water portal: stepping into a puddle and emerging in a different world

### Super Speed
**Prompt:** A person stands still in a busy city street. They take a step — and they're suddenly 100 feet away. The world around them is frozen. A pigeon hangs in mid-flight. Rain drops are suspended. A person's surprised expression is frozen. The speedster walks through the frozen scene, examining details — a raindrop on a leaf, a bee's wings mid-flap. They snap their fingers. Time resumes — but they're already gone. Everyone looks around, confused. A gust of wind blows through where they were standing. The pigeon finally lands. One person notices their coffee has been moved to a different table.

**Settings:** 16:9, 4 seconds

**Expected result:** Time-freeze superpower. The frozen world creates a diorama effect — every suspended object is a mini-set-piece. The moved coffee is the comedic Easter egg.

**Variations:**
- Speed run: a blur streak through a city, buildings streaking past
- Speed typing: fingers a blur, words appearing on screen instantly
- Speed cooking: ingredients flying, dish assembled in a second

### Levitation Scene
**Prompt:** A person sits cross-legged, eyes closed, meditating. They begin to float — slowly, steadily. Their clothes sway with the movement. Small objects around them lift off too: books, pens, a coffee mug, a phone. Everything orbits the person in a slow ring. The person rises above the floor, above the desk, nearly to the ceiling. Their eyes open — they're glowing faintly blue. The objects orbit faster. They make a subtle hand gesture — the objects freeze in place. They lower themselves gently back to the floor. Objects set down softly. The phone lands face-up: it shows a text message that says "u good?"

**Settings:** 9:16, 4 seconds

**Expected result:** Telekinesis made calm and meditative, not violent. The floating objects create a mobile/solar system effect. The final text message is the modern comedy beat.

**Variations:**
- Emotional: anger causes objects to fly chaotically, smash
- Healing: green energy flows from hands, wounds closing
- Teleportation: person blinks out, leaves behind only their clothes (for a frame)

## Tips: Describing Physics-Defying Effects in Pika

### How to Describe Impossible Effects
- **Be specific about the physics**: "Water flows upward" not "water goes weird"
- **Describe the transition**: "Ice forms on the surface, spreading from left to right" not "it freezes"
- **Include environmental reactions**: "Wind blows from the portal, papers scatter"
- **Define the scope**: "Only the glass breaks — everything else is unaffected"

### Effect Keywords for Pika
- **Slow motion**: "extreme slow motion," "time frozen," "suspended in air"
- **Reverse**: "in reverse," "time reversal," "un-doing"
- **Scale shift**: "miniature to full-size," "zooming into," "macro to micro"
- **Morph**: "transforming into," "morphing from X to Y," "dissolving into"
- **Physics-defying**: "floating," "levitating," "defying gravity," "impossible geometry"

### Keeping Effects Realistic (Even When They're Not)
- Include real-world physics anchors: "steam rises," "shadows cast," "reflections visible"
- Reference real phenomena: "like a soap bubble" or "like liquid mercury"
- Describe the aftermath: "the floor is wet," "smoke lingers," "shards remain"
- Include sound cues: "a crack," "a hum," "silence" — even if there's no audio, it affects the visual

### Common Pitfalls
- Too many simultaneous effects: Pika handles 1-2 effects per clip best
- Vague descriptions: "cool effects" produces random results
- Conflicting physics: "fire and water coexisting in the same space" needs careful description
- Overlong descriptions: Keep effects prompts focused on one transformation
