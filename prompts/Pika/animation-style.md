## 2D Animation

### Looney Tunes Chase
**Prompt:** A cartoon rabbit (classic Looney Tunes style, not a specific character) runs through a desert landscape, leaving dust clouds. A cartoon coyote (generic) chases behind, legs spinning in a circular blur before gaining traction. The rabbit rounds a corner and paints a tunnel on a rock wall. The rabbit runs through the painted tunnel. The coyote tries to follow — SPLAT, hits the rock. The coyote peels off the wall, accordion-style. Classic cel animation style: bold outlines, saturated flat colors, squash-and-stretch physics. 24fps traditional animation feel.

**Settings:** 16:9, 4 seconds

**Expected result:** Classic cartoon physics comedy. The painted tunnel gag is timeless. The animation style should look hand-drawn, not digital. Flat colors, expressive poses, exaggerated movement.

**Variations:**
- Roadrunner-style: the coyote orders from ACME, device backfires
- Tom and Jerry-style: house chase, doors slamming, hiding in impossible places
- Modern style: same gag, but with contemporary flat-design animation aesthetic

### Paper Cutout Stop-Motion
**Prompt:** A paper cutout forest scene. Trees are layered green paper, cut into leaf shapes. A paper fox (orange with a white belly) walks through the forest. Its legs move in a walking cycle — flipbook style, 8 frames per step. Behind it, paper flowers "grow" from the ground — they pop up one at a time, spring-loaded. A paper sun moves across a paper sky (visible paper texture, slightly wrinkled). The fox stops at a paper river (blue cellophane, crinkled to look like water). It drinks. A paper bird (white, simple shape) lands on its head. Everything has visible paper texture, slight shadows from the cutout edges, and a warm, handcrafted feel.

**Settings:** 16:9, 4 seconds

**Expected result:** Charming stop-motion paper animation. Every element has visible paper texture and cut edges. The limited movement (flipbook-style) adds to the handmade aesthetic. Wes Anderson's "Fantastic Mr. Fox" meets children's book.

**Variations:**
- Ocean scene: layered blue paper waves, paper fish, paper boat
- City: paper buildings, paper cars moving on paper roads
- Night: paper moon, paper stars (silver foil), paper owl

### Retro 80s Cartoon Intro
**Prompt:** A neon-outlined character (generic action hero, big hair, headband) runs through a grid of neon lines receding to infinity (Tron-style). Behind them, an explosion — rendered in orange, red, and yellow with speed lines. The character leaps and strikes a pose mid-air. A title card appears in chrome 3D lettering with a lens flare. Everything has scan lines and VHS tracking artifacts. The color palette: hot pink, electric blue, chrome silver, neon green. Synth-wave aesthetic. The animation is slightly stiff — intentionally, like a 1980s Saturday morning cartoon.

**Settings:** 16:9, 4 seconds

**Expected result:** Nostalgic 80s cartoon aesthetic. VHS artifacts, neon colors, chrome text, and intentionally limited animation. The synth-wave vibe is key. "He-Man" meets "Stranger Things" credits.

**Variations:**
- 90s X-Men style: dramatic poses, extreme angles, leather and pouches
- 70s psychedelic: fluid colors, morphing shapes, Yellow Submarine style
- Japanese 80s anime: Akira-style shading, motorcycle, Neo-Tokyo

### Flipbook Animation
**Prompt:** A hand flips through a flipbook — visible at the edge of each page. The flipbook shows a flower growing: seed → sprout → stem → leaves → bud → bloom → wilt → seed falls → new sprout. The cycle of life in 60 pages. Each page is a pencil sketch on off-white paper with visible page edges. The hand flips faster as the flower grows, slower as it wilts. The final page: the seed falling to the ground, and the hand starts flipping backward — the flower un-wilts, un-blooms, and returns to a seed. The flipbook loops.

**Settings:** 9:16, 4 seconds (loop)

**Expected result:** Charmingly analog animation format. The visible flipbook edges and pencil sketches create warmth. The life cycle is the content; the loop-back is the magic trick.

**Variations:**
- Bird flight: wing cycle, takeoff, soaring, landing
- Human figure: walking cycle, then running, then dancing
- Day/night: sun rises, moves across sky, sets, moon rises

## 3D Render

### Pixar-Style Character Reaction
**Prompt:** A Pixar-style 3D rendered character (round, soft, expressive eyes, simple design — think a sentient toaster) discovers a piece of bread for the first time. The character's eyes widen (pupils dilate). It approaches cautiously. It pokes the bread — the bread squishes realistically. The character's expression shifts from curiosity to wonder. It looks at its own slot (it's a toaster, after all). Back at the bread. Back at the slot. A decision is made. It reaches for the bread with a fork. The lighting: Pixar's signature three-point with a warm key and cool fill. Subsurface scattering on the plastic body. Ambient occlusion in every crease.

**Settings:** 16:9, 4 seconds

**Expected result:** Emotionally expressive 3D character with Pixar-quality rendering. The "personality from objects" approach. The eyes do 90% of the acting. Warm, inviting, technically polished.

**Variations:**
- Sad robot: a broken robot in a junkyard, one eye flickering, a flower growing from its chest
- Excited book: a book that opens itself, pages fluttering with excitement
- Nervous lightbulb: a lightbulb about to be switched on, terrified of the dark

### Low-Poly World
**Prompt:** A low-poly landscape rendered in minimal geometric shapes. Mountains: simple triangular meshes, flat-shaded in purple and blue. Trees: cone shapes in green, trunk is a cylinder. A river: flat blue polygon with subtle vertex animation. Clouds: puffy polyhedra. A low-poly fox (30 polygons, flat-shaded orange) walks through the scene. The camera orbits the landscape slowly. The entire world has visible polygon edges — no smoothing, no texture maps. Pure geometric form. The sun is a glowing yellow octahedron. The color palette is limited: 8 colors total.

**Settings:** 16:9, 4 seconds

**Expected result:** Minimalist 3D world where every polygon is visible and intentional. The constraint of low-poly creates a distinctive, charming aesthetic. Feels like a video game lobby.

**Variations:**
- Low-poly city: buildings are rectangular prisms, cars are box shapes
- Low-poly ocean: waves are angular mesh, fish are flat triangles
- Low-poly space: planets are icosahedra, stars are tetrahedra

### Claymation-Style Characters
**Prompt:** Two claymation characters (clay texture, visible thumbprints) sit at a small table. Character A is round, blue, and has a tall hat. Character B is thin, red, and has glasses. Character A slides a cup of tea across the table. Character B picks it up — the clay hand deforms slightly around the cup (realistic clay physics). Character B takes a sip and their eyes go wide — it's too hot. Steam comes out of their ears (classic cartoon gag, claymation execution). Character A laughs — their whole body jiggles (clay wobble). The table has visible clay crumbs. The background: a clay window showing a clay garden. Everything has fingerprint texture.

**Settings:** 16:9, 4 seconds

**Expected result:** Aardman/Wallace & Gromit quality claymation. Visible fingerprints and clay deformation are key. The humor is gentle and physical. The steam-from-ears gag works because it's clay.

**Variations:**
- Monster creation: clay being shaped into a creature, the creature comes alive
- Kitchen chaos: clay food rolling off a clay counter
- Race: clay snails, one wins by a shell

## Anime

### Shonen Power-Up
**Prompt:** An anime character (generic shonen protagonist — spiky hair, determined eyes, torn gi) stands in a crater. Energy gathers around them — visible as glowing blue-white particles spiraling inward. Their hair begins to float upward. The ground cracks beneath their feet in a radial pattern. Energy aura intensifies — from flickering sparks to a solid column of light. Their eyes flash. The camera spins around them at increasing speed. The energy output: shockwave rings expanding outward. The character screams (mouth open, veins visible on forehead). The power-up completes: steady aura, calm expression, absolute confidence. Classic shonen anime style: bold outlines, speed lines, dramatic lighting.

**Settings:** 9:16, 4 seconds

**Expected result:** The iconic anime power-up scene. Escalating energy, spinning camera, ground destruction. Dragon Ball Z meets Naruto. Over-the-top, dramatic, inspiring.

**Variations:**
- Villain reveal: dark energy, red aura, sinister smile
- Awakening: character opens eyes, reveals a new power (sharingan, gear, etc.)
- Team power-up: multiple characters powering up simultaneously, colors matching

### Ghibli Meal Scene
**Prompt:** In the style of Studio Ghibli's food scenes: a steaming bowl of rice with grilled fish, pickled vegetables, and miso soup. The steam rises in curling, animated tendrils. A hand picks up chopsticks — the movement is Ghibli's signature "realistic anticipation" (slight pause before the action). The chopsticks pick up rice — each grain is individually rendered. The character (seen from chin down, Ghibli-style) brings the rice to their mouth. Their cheeks bulge slightly as they chew. A satisfied nod. The miso soup is sipped — the bowl tilts, steam touching the character's face. The background: a warm kitchen, afternoon light, a cat sleeping on the counter.

**Settings:** 16:9, 4 seconds

**Expected result:** The legendary Ghibli food animation — the most appetizing food in all of animation history. Every Ghibli meal scene makes you hungry. This captures that magic: the steam, the detail, the warmth.

**Variations:**
- Bento box: opening a wrapped bento, colorful compartments revealed
- Bakery: fresh bread coming out of oven, flour dust, warm glow
- Ramen shop: noodles being pulled, broth ladled, toppings placed

### Makoto Shinkai Sky
**Prompt:** An anime sky in the style of Makoto Shinkai — the most beautiful sky imaginable. Clouds are rendered with impossible detail: each layer has texture, translucency, and color gradients. The sky transitions from deep cerulean blue at the zenith to warm peach at the horizon. Sun rays pierce through cloud gaps in Shinkai's signature god-ray style. A contrail from a plane cuts across the sky, dissolving slowly. The camera tilts up from a rooftop (visible railing at the bottom of frame). A flock of birds crosses the sky in formation. The clouds are in slow motion — they drift, morph, and catch light differently every second. The level of detail exceeds what any anime "should" have.

**Settings:** 16:9, 4 seconds

**Expected result:** Makoto Shinkai's sky — the reason people pause his films and screenshot the backgrounds. Clouds that look more real than real. God rays that feel spiritual. A sky that makes you feel something.

**Variations:**
- Sunset: impossibly warm oranges, pinks, purples, silhouetted cityscape
- Storm approaching: dramatic dark clouds, wind visible in hair and clothes
- Night: Shinkai's star fields, shooting star, moon with visible craters

## Pixel Art

### Retro Game Scene
**Prompt:** A 16-bit pixel art character (16 pixels tall, retro RPG style) walks through a fantasy forest. Trees are tall, detailed pixel sprites with visible leaves. The character's walk cycle is 4 frames — smooth but distinctly retro. A treasure chest appears. The character opens it — a sparkle animation (8-pixel star burst). A sword is obtained — the character holds it up (classic "item obtained" pose). A slime enemy appears from behind a tree. The character attacks — a slash animation with a white arc. The slime splits into two smaller slimes. The background: parallax scrolling forest layers, each at a different speed. Scanline overlay for CRT effect.

**Settings:** 16:9, 4 seconds

**Expected result:** Authentic 16-bit RPG experience. Pixel-perfect art, classic game animations, CRT scanline effect. The entire sequence feels like a cutscene from a SNES game. Nostalgia-inducing.

**Variations:**
- Space shooter: pixel ship, laser bullets, alien enemies, explosion sprites
- Platformer: character jumps across gaps, collects coins, avoids spikes
- Town scene: NPCs walking, shop signs, day-night cycle via palette shift

### Minecraft-Style Build
**Prompt:** A Minecraft-style block world. Blocks are placed one at a time — each placement has a satisfying "click" sound. A house is being built: dirt foundation, wooden walls, glass windows, a door. The build accelerates — walls go up faster, roof tiles fly into place. Interior furnishing: crafting table, furnace, bed, torches on walls (the torchlight flickers). The camera orbits the house as it's built. The final touch: a torch is placed on the front porch. The camera pulls back to show the house in its biome: a meadow with block flowers, block sheep, and a block sun setting behind block mountains. The pixel/block aesthetic is consistent.

**Settings:** 16:9, 4 seconds

**Expected result:** Satisfying building timelapse in Minecraft aesthetic. Each block placement is visible and satisfying. The house grows organically. The final pull-back shows the world context.

**Variations:**
- Redstone contraption: pistons, repeaters, and redstone dust connecting
- Castle build: massive scale, towers, walls, flags
- Underground base: mining into stone, furnishing a cave

## Tips: Describing Animation Styles in Pika

### Style Keywords That Work
- **2D**: "2D animation," "cel animation," "hand-drawn," "flat color," "bold outlines"
- **3D**: "3D render," "Pixar style," "Blender render," "low-poly," "stylized 3D"
- **Anime**: "anime style," "Studio Ghibli," "shonen," "shoujo," "mecha anime"
- **Pixel**: "pixel art," "8-bit," "16-bit," "retro game," "sprite animation"
- **Stop-motion**: "claymation," "stop motion," "paper cutout," "felt animation"
- **Other**: "flipbook," "rotoscope," "motion comics," "vector animation"

### Character Consistency Tips
- Describe the character fully in the first prompt
- Reference specific visual traits: "blue hair, red scarf, round glasses"
- Keep the same style keywords across related prompts
- Simpler characters are more consistent than detailed ones

### Common Pitfalls
- Requesting smooth 3D and hand-drawn 2D in the same prompt (conflicting)
- Asking for specific copyrighted characters (use generic descriptions)
- Too many characters in one shot (Pika handles 1-3 best)
- Complex camera movements in animation (keep it simpler than live-action)
