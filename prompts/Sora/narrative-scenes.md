> Tool: Sora (OpenAI) | Style: Narrative / Story-driven | Last updated: 2026

## Drama

### The Letter Never Sent
**Prompt:** Close-up of an elderly woman's hands trembling as she holds a yellowed envelope. Camera slowly pulls back to reveal her sitting alone at a kitchen table in a small apartment. Morning light through lace curtains. She turns the envelope over — it's addressed in faded ink, never stamped. Her eyes well up. She places it gently into a wooden box with other unsent letters. The camera lingers on her face — decades of regret in a single expression. Naturalistic lighting, muted color palette, shot on 35mm Kodak 250D. No dialogue. Piano score only.

**Settings:** 16:9, 20 seconds

**Expected result:** Quiet, emotionally devastating character moment. The audience understands the weight of unsaid words without a single line of dialogue. "Amour" meets "The Remains of the Day."

**Variations:**
- She opens the letter and we see her younger self writing it (flashback cut)
- Her adult child enters, sees the box, and sits down silently beside her
- She finally mails it: cut to her walking to a mailbox in the rain

### Hospital Waiting Room
**Prompt:** Medium shot of a man in his 40s sitting in a hospital waiting room. He's in work clothes — construction dust on his boots. He stares at the linoleum floor, hands clasped. A child's drawing is folded in his shirt pocket, peeking out. Other patients sit in the background, blurred. Fluorescent overhead light creates unflattering shadows. The clock on the wall moves in slow time-lapse — 2 hours compressed to 15 seconds. His expression doesn't change. A nurse enters from the right, says something (no audio), and his face transforms from dread to relief. He exhales. Camera pushes in slowly on his face.

**Settings:** 16:9, 20 seconds

**Expected result:** Visceral emotional journey compressed into a single waiting room scene. The time-lapse clock and his unchanging expression build tension, released by the nurse's arrival.

**Variations:**
- Opposite outcome: the nurse shakes her head, and he collapses forward
- Night shift version: empty waiting room, harsher lighting, more isolated
- His wife enters from the hallway, they embrace wordlessly

### Father and Son at the Dock
**Prompt:** Wide shot of a weathered wooden dock extending into a still lake at dawn. A father (50s, gray beard, worn flannel) teaches his adult son (20s, reluctant) to tie a fishing knot. Close-up on their hands — his father's rough, scarred; his son's smooth, uncertain. The son struggles. The father gently takes the line and guides his son's fingers. No words spoken. Camera alternates between close-ups on hands and wider shots of their body language. The son finally ties the knot. The father nods once. They cast their lines in silence. Shot on ARRI Alexa, 50mm, shallow depth of field. Morning mist on the water.

**Settings:** 16:9, 20 seconds

**Expected result:** A masterclass in visual storytelling about generational connection. Every emotion conveyed through hands and silence. Terrence Malick energy.

**Variations:**
- Flash forward: the son now older, teaching his own child the same knot
- The dock is in winter: ice fishing, same dynamic, colder palette
- Add a dog sitting between them, tail wagging gently

### Rain Through a Window
**Prompt:** Interior shot. A woman sits at a desk facing a rain-streaked window. Her reflection and the rainy street outside overlap in the glass. She's writing something, pauses, crosses it out. Outside: people with umbrellas, a bus passing, city life continuing. She watches a couple share an umbrella across the street. She looks down at what she's writing — it's a resignation letter. She folds it, puts it in her bag, and picks up her phone. Her expression shifts: a small, determined smile. She stands up, grabs her coat. Camera follows her to the door. Cut to: exterior, she walks out into the rain without an umbrella, chin up, smiling.

**Settings:** 16:9, 20 seconds

**Expected result:** Interior monologue told entirely through reflection, body language, and one decisive moment of courage. The rain metaphor works on multiple levels.

**Variations:**
- She tears up the letter and keeps working — a story about acceptance instead
- The couple she watches is arguing — she chooses differently
- Snow instead of rain, different city, same emotional arc

## Comedy

### The Impossible IKEA Assembly
**Prompt:** A increasingly frustrated man (30s, glasses, t-shirt that says "I can do it myself") tries to assemble an IKEA bookshelf in his living room. Time-lapse style: he starts confident, reading the manual with a smug smile. Quick cuts: wrong piece, backwards panel, leftover screws (lots of leftover screws). His expression degrades: confusion, frustration, bargaining with the furniture. He's now surrounded by wooden dowels and Allen keys like a defeated king. His cat watches from the couch, judging. Final shot: the bookshelf is slightly crooked, leaning left. He places a single book on it. It collapses. He stares at camera. Cut to black.

**Settings:** 16:9, 20 seconds

**Expected result:** Universal comedy of DIY failure. Escalating frustration played for physical comedy. The cat's judgment is the punchline.

**Variations:**
- His partner silently assembles the same shelf in the background while he struggles
- He calls a professional: a person arrives, assembles it in 10 seconds, leaves
- Version 2: he tries to return it, IKEA maze layout makes him lost for hours

### The Dog Who Stole Thanksgiving
**Prompt:** A golden retriever sits perfectly still in the dining room, pretending to be a good boy. The camera captures his POV as his eyes slowly track a Thanksgiving turkey on the kitchen counter. His owners are in the next room watching football. Cut to: the dog "casually" walks to the kitchen. Cut to: owners hear a crash. Cut to: the dog is under the dining table, turkey carcass between his paws, surrounded by torn napkins. His face: zero guilt, maximum satisfaction. A single piece of cranberry sauce is on his nose. Owners stare in disbelief. The dog wags his tail once.

**Settings:** 16:9, 15 seconds

**Expected result:** Physical comedy told from the dog's perspective. The buildup of "good boy" innocence makes the payoff funnier. The cranberry sauce on the nose is the comedic button.

**Variations:**
- Cat version: the cat knocks the turkey off the counter with zero hesitation
- The dog tries to hide evidence by pushing scraps under the rug
- Slow-motion "Ocean's Eleven" style heist version with dramatic music

### The Zoom Meeting Disaster
**Prompt:** Split-screen: left side is a professional Zoom meeting with 6 serious faces. Right side is a single participant's home — he's in a suit jacket and boxers, trying to look professional. His cat jumps on his desk. He pushes it off. His toddler wanders in wearing a pot as a helmet. He whispers "go to mommy" while maintaining a corporate smile. The toddler waves at the camera. The meeting participants try not to laugh. A delivery person rings his doorbell — the dog goes berserk. He mutes himself, chaos visible on his face. Unmutes: "Sorry, I was on mute." Everyone knows. Nobody says anything.

**Settings:** 16:9, 20 seconds

**Expected result:** Relatable remote work comedy. The contrast between professional facade and domestic chaos is the core joke. Escalating interruptions build the comedy.

**Variations:**
- His virtual background fails, revealing the messy room behind him
- He accidentally shares his screen showing his online shopping cart
- Everyone else's cameras reveal their own chaos — solidarity moment

## Thriller / Suspense

### The Elevator
**Prompt:** Interior of a modern office building elevator. A woman in a business suit enters alone. She presses floor 12. The doors close. The elevator starts ascending. At floor 7, it stops. Doors open — nobody there. She presses close. At floor 9, it stops again. Doors open — the hallway is dark, emergency lights only. She presses close repeatedly. The doors finally close. She looks at her phone — no signal. The elevator passes floor 12. It doesn't stop. Floor 15. 18. 20. The building only has 20 floors. The number display glitches. She backs against the wall. The elevator stops at a floor that doesn't exist: **-1**. The doors open. Blackness. She doesn't move. Something moves in the darkness. The doors close. She's alone again. Her floor: 12. Normal. She exits. Her reflection in the polished lobby floor shows someone standing behind her. Nobody is there.

**Settings:** 9:16 (vertical for phone), 20 seconds

**Expected result:** Creeping dread that escalates through small wrong details. Each floor adds a new impossibility. The reflection ending leaves the threat unresolved. "The Shining" meets modern corporate.

**Variations:**
- She gets on with a stranger who disappears between floors
- The elevator is a glass exterior elevator — she can see the city shrinking below
- Reverse: she starts at -1 and the "real world" is the lie

### The Photograph
**Prompt:** A man in a darkroom develops photographs. Red safe-light bathes the scene. He hangs a photo of a street scene to dry. He notices something in the photo he didn't see when shooting: a figure in a window across the street is watching him — through a camera lens. He picks up another photo from the same roll: same figure, different window, closer. Next photo: the figure is on the street, still looking at him. Next: the figure is closer, slightly blurred — walking toward the camera. Final photo in the roll: it's a close-up of the man's own face, sleeping, shot from inside his bedroom. He drops the photo. Camera slowly tilts up from the photo on the floor to the darkroom door — it's open. It was closed before.

**Settings:** 16:9, 20 seconds

**Expected result:** Hitchcockian surveillance paranoia told through the physical medium of photography. Each photo reveals more, building dread. The final reveal — the open door — confirms the threat is present.

**Variations:**
- Digital version: scrolling through photos on a laptop, same escalation
- The photos show future events, not past ones
- She discovers she's been photographing herself without memory of it

### The Passenger
**Prompt:** POV from the passenger seat of a car driving down a deserted highway at night. The driver is calm, humming to the radio. The passenger notices: the gas gauge is on empty. The clock reads 3:47 AM. The road signs don't match any real place. They pass the same barn with a red door three times — the camera confirms this by showing it in the window reflection each time. The passenger points this out. The driver smiles and says something (no audio — the passenger can't hear words). The passenger looks at the side mirror: headlights behind them, getting closer. The driver accelerates. The headlights match their speed exactly. The passenger looks at the driver — for one frame, the driver's face is wrong. Normal the next frame. The road ahead forks. The driver takes the left fork without hesitation. A sign reads "NO EXIT."

**Settings:** 16:9, 20 seconds

**Available result:** "Twilight Zone" style wrongness that accumulates. The passenger is trapped in a loop they can't escape. The one-frame face glitch is the horror.

**Variations:**
- The passenger is the ghost: they died in the car and the driver is taking them somewhere
- The car is autonomous — there is no driver
- They arrive at a gas station. Everyone there looks like the driver.

## Sci-Fi

### The Last Human Museum
**Prompt:** A humanoid robot curator walks through a vast, cathedral-like museum 10,000 years in the future. Exhibits contain preserved human artifacts: a smartphone, a coffee mug, a child's drawing, a vinyl record. The robot pauses at each, handling them with extreme care, as if they're holy relics. Camera follows from behind, smooth tracking shot. The robot picks up a faded photograph of a human family — it studies the faces. Its optical sensors focus and refocus. It places the photo back with deliberate tenderness. It looks up at a massive inscription on the wall: "HOMO SAPIENS: 300,000 BCE — 3,024 CE." The robot's head tilts slightly — an unmistakable gesture of something that might be grief.

**Settings:** 2.39:1, 20 seconds

**Available result:** Post-human elegy told through objects. The robot's reverence for human artifacts implies what was lost. The inscription provides the date context. "Wall-E" meets "Blade Runner 2049."

**Variations:**
- A human child enters — unfrozen from cryosleep — and the robot tries to explain what humans were
- The museum is in ruins: nature reclaiming the artifacts
- The robot activates a preserved human hologram and "talks" to it

### First Contact Through a Window
**Prompt:** Interior of a space station observation module. An astronaut floats in zero gravity, looking out the window at Earth. A strange light appears outside — not a satellite, not a star. It moves with purpose. It stops 50ft from the window. The astronaut presses their hand against the glass. The light pulses. On the other side of the glass, something presses back — not a hand, but something that matches the hand's shape. The astronaut's breath fogs the glass. The light entity mimics the fog pattern, creating its own condensation. Communication without language. The astronaut smiles, tears floating in zero-g. Camera holds on their face — wonder, fear, and joy simultaneously.

**Settings:** 16:9, 20 seconds

**Expected result:** First contact told through the most human possible gesture — a hand on glass. The zero-g tears are the emotional peak. "Arrival" meets "2001."

**Variations:**
- The entity enters the station: warm light fills the module
- Multiple light entities appear, surrounding the station
- The astronaut is the last person alive on the station — this is both salvation and farewell

### Memory Download
**Prompt:** Close-up of a person's face as they sit in a futuristic memory chair — neural interface contacts on their temples, soft blue UI holograms floating around their head. Their eyes are closed but rapidly moving. We see their memories projected as flickering holograms around them: childhood birthday party, a beach sunset, a first kiss, a car accident, a hospital room, a graduation. The memories speed up, overlapping, degrading. The person's expression shifts with each — joy, fear, love, pain. Suddenly all memories freeze, then shatter like glass. The person opens their eyes. They don't recognize the room. They look at their hands as if seeing them for the first time. Memory wiped. Clean slate. What do you do with a second chance?

**Settings:** 16:9, 20 seconds

**Available result:** Sci-fi emotional thriller about memory and identity. The hologram memory projections are visually striking. The final moment — alienation from one's own body — is haunting.

**Variations:**
- They chose to erase the memories: we see them pressing "confirm" first
- The memories belong to someone else — they're living a borrowed life
- One memory survives: a face they can't place, but it makes them cry

## Fantasy

### The Library of Unwritten Books
**Prompt:** A child pushes open an enormous wooden door into a library that stretches infinitely in all directions — up, down, sideways, in directions that don't exist in normal geometry. Every shelf is filled with glowing books. Each book's spine shows a title and the words "NEVER WRITTEN." The child pulls one out: it's warm to the touch, pulsing with faint light. They open it — the pages are blank at first, then a story begins to write itself in real-time ink, illustrating the child's own life choices. The child reads ahead and sees a future they haven't lived yet. They close the book quickly, but it's too late — the ink is already spreading to their skin, becoming part of them. Shot in a Studio Ghibli meets Guillermo del Toro style: whimsical but with undercurrents of danger.

**Settings:** 16:9, 20 seconds

**Expected result:** Fantasy architecture that defies physics, warm magical lighting, and a story-within-a-story structure. The ink-on-skin transformation is the magical consequence.

**Variations:**
- The child finds a book titled with their own name — it's the only one with an ending already written
- An old librarian appears: they're the author of all these unwritten books
- The library is dying: books are crumbling to dust, and one is the cure

### The Last Dragon's Cave
**Prompt:** Deep inside a mountain cavern, lit by bioluminescent crystals and rivers of molten gold. A dragon — ancient, enormous, scarred — lies curled around a clutch of eggs. It's the last of its kind. A young girl (8 years old, dirt-smudged face, torn clothes) enters the cave holding a lantern. The dragon raises its head, fire building in its throat. The girl doesn't flinch. She sets the lantern down and sits cross-legged. She pulls out a tattered book and begins reading aloud. The dragon's fire recedes. It lowers its head. Its massive eye — the size of the girl — blinks slowly. She keeps reading. The dragon's breathing slows. One of the eggs cracks. The girl pauses, looks at the crack, and smiles. She keeps reading.

**Settings:** 16:9, 20 seconds

**Expected result:** A scene about the power of stories to tame even the most terrifying creature. The scale contrast between girl and dragon is key. "How to Train Your Dragon" meets "The Neverending Story."

**Variations:**
- The book is about the dragon's own history — she's reading the dragon its forgotten past
- The girl is the dragon in human form — a magical identity reveal
- The cave is actually a crashed spaceship, and the "dragon" is an alien

### Painting a World Into Existence
**Prompt:** An artist sits before a blank white canvas in a white void. They dip a brush in paint — the brush touches the canvas and the paint bleeds off the canvas into reality. A single brushstroke of blue creates an ocean that spreads across the floor. Green strokes grow into trees that rise from the void. The artist paints faster — mountains erupt, clouds form, rain falls from nothing. The world they're creating starts pushing back: waves crash at their feet, wind blows their hair. They paint a sun. Light floods everything. They hesitate, then paint a figure — a person, standing on the new shore, looking back at them. The artist and their creation lock eyes. The artist sets down the brush and steps into the painting. Camera pulls back: the canvas is now a window into a living world.

**Settings:** 16:9, 20 seconds

**Expected result:** Meta-narrative about creation itself. The transition from blank void to living world happens through the act of painting. The final pull-back recontextualizes the entire scene.

**Variations:**
- The creation fights back: what they paint doesn't want to be controlled
- Another artist enters, painting over the first artist's work — creative conflict
- The canvas has been painting itself the whole time, and the artist is what it created

## Tips: How to Write Narrative Sora Prompts

### Describing Character Emotions
- Don't just say "sad" — describe the physical manifestation: "her breath catches," "his jaw tightens," "tears pool but don't fall"
- Use contradiction: "smiling, but her eyes are empty"
- Include micro-expressions: "a flicker of recognition crosses his face for half a second"

### Pacing and Transitions
- **Slow burns**: "Camera holds on the face for a full 10 seconds before anything happens"
- **Quick cuts**: "Rapid montage — 2-second cuts, each a different memory"
- **Time compression**: "Time-lapse: seasons change outside the window while she sits motionless"
- **Match cuts**: "Cut from the spinning wheel to a spinning ceiling fan to a spinning galaxy"

### Show, Don't Tell
- Replace dialogue with visual action: instead of "he says goodbye," write "he places his key on the table and walks out without looking back"
- Use objects as emotional anchors: "the coffee mug she always used, now sitting empty and clean"
- Let the environment reflect internal state: "the walls are closing in — literally, the room is shrinking by 1 inch per second"

### Camera as Narrator
- **Push in** on important details: "Camera slowly pushes in on the letter in her hand"
- **Pull back** for revelation: "Camera pulls back to reveal she's not in a room — she's in a cell"
- **Hold** for emotional weight: "Camera holds on the empty chair for 5 seconds after he leaves"
- **Tilt/pan** for discovery: "Camera tilts down from the sky to reveal what she's standing on"
